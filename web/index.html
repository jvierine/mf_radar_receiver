Quick-look method

From voltage to SNR

  1. Correct the complex voltage. The known USRP digital-downconverter offset, −0.25−0.25i, is removed from every sample.
  2. Form received power. Five consecutive 10 ms interpulse periods are converted to |z|² and averaged incoherently.
  3. Combine the dipoles. Power from channels 1, 3, and 4 is averaged. The broadband loop channel is excluded from this quick-look product.
  4. Reduce the range resolution. Ten adjacent 1 MHz voltage samples are averaged in power, producing approximately 1.5 km one-way range bins.
  5. Estimate the noise floor. For each one-minute profile, the 20th percentile of power between 250 and 1400 km is used as a robust receiver-noise estimate.
  6. Calculate SNR. Noise power is subtracted before division: the displayed value is 10 log₁₀[(P − Pnoise) / Pnoise]. Non-positive estimates fall below the plotted scale. The mesospheric view is fixed at −3 to 20 dB; the full-range view is fixed at −3 to 35 dB.

One profile is retained per minute over a rolling 48-hour window. This is an operational quick-look: it does not use coherent integration, matched filtering, phase calibration, beamforming, or Doppler selection.
